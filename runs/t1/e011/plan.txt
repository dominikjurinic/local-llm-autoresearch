I've been over-thinking this without a clean, testable direction. Let me commit to one idea. The diagnostics are clear: wrong-class assignment on overlapping boxes costs ~0.11 mAP, dominated by pore (62%) and deposit/stain confusion (77%/89%). The remaining ranking/scoring error is only ~0.015 mAP. So the entire bottleneck is the classifier's accuracy on these boxes — especially for small defects.

**Why the current model struggles:** it fuses tight (1.5x) and wide (2.5x) crops at a *fixed* 160px resolution, with per-class logit weights learned from ground-truth boxes. At test time it classifies detector boxes whose sizes vary widely. Small defects are much harder (80% vs 91-92% for medium/large). The current per-class fusion is too coarse — it can't adapt per-box to the actual context a detector box provides, and the fixed 160px means small defects don't fill enough of the crop.

**A genuinely new idea:** per-box adaptive fusion weights and adaptive crop contexts. Rather than one fixed tight/wide pair for every box, use box-size-adaptive context (small boxes → more magnified tight crop, larger wide crop) plus per-box learned fusion (a small MLP taking box size/aspect ratio + detector conf as input and outputting the fusion weight). This directly targets "small 80% correct vs medium/large 91-92%" — a pattern no earlier family addressed. It's also computationally cheap: one extra small MLP, same two forward passes.

**Budget:** the current run finishes at ~162s within the 180s budget (RESERVE_S=55). The per-box MLP adds negligible compute; the adaptive crop change requires no extra forward passes. Margin is safe.

**How it differs from earlier experiments:** not another backbone, not another augmentation family, not just detector-box training data — this changes what the model *sees* and *how it fuses*, adapting to each box's geometry. The "third wide view" family proved per-class context weighting helps (+0.005); e010 crashed on an environment issue (triton/gcc build) before showing results; here I make context adaptive *per-box* with a cheap MLP, which should hit the small-defect error hardest.
