Looking at the history, I can group the experiments:
- **Data changes** (e001+, e006~): Adding low-res helped; detector-box replacement and false-alarm training didn't.
- **Architecture/crop changes** (e003-, e004~, e005-): Single crop, smaller images, and schedule tweaks all failed. The two-crop setup at 160px is solid.
- **Sampling/scoring** (e007-, e009-): Class duplication and top-1 scoring hurt.
- **Augmentation** (e010~): Rotation/contrast was a wash.

What hasn't been tried: **changing the backbone itself**. Every architecture experiment so far modified how the model sees the input (crops, resolution), not the model's feature extractor. The loss is still falling at 0.31 after 2 epochs, but e005 showed that simply getting more steps via smaller images doesn't help—the model needs better features per step, not more of the same.

`tf_efficientnetv2_b0` is the natural upgrade: same family, pretrained weights available, and it uses FusedMBConv blocks with a more efficient depthwise-separable design that should extract better local texture features (critical for distinguishing pore vs. deposit/stain) at comparable compute. It's a one-line change with a clear, testable hypothesis.
