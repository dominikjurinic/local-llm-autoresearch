HYPOTHESIS: The dominant loss (~0.10 mAP) is pore→stain and deposit→stain confusion on true defects. A "large" box covering most of a real stain can be classified from coarse shape, but pore/deposit misread as stain suggests the classifier is really relying on weak global cues (box area, overall intensity) instead of fine texture. I hypothesise that forcing every training example through a fixed 1.0× tight crop (the defect at full size) alongside the multi-scale pyramid will give the model a dedicated high-resolution view of the defect itself, which is where the distinguishing texture lives and where all four classes genuinely differ.
Differences from e011/e019: those tried plain resolution increase or a second stream and crashed/timed out because EfficientNet-B0 at 256px with 4 views OOM'd; here the change is an architecture-level fusion that adds one tight crop without raising any other input size, keeping the time budget intact.
Expected gain: +0.015–0.03 mAP on the two weak classes if the tight crop re-anchors the classifier on actual defect texture rather than context heuristics.
Time-budget note: 5 views at 160 px adds ~25% to both step time and prediction time. Current run: 13 s load + 82 s train (157 steps) + 73 s pred = 168 s. New estimate: 13 s + ~67 s train (~110 steps at 0.61 s/step) + ~91 s pred = ~171 s. RESERVE_S raised 85→100 to keep a ≥9 s safety margin.
